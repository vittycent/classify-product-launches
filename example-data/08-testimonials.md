## 3. Review site, posted 9 December 2025, verified reviewer

**Rating: 5 / 5 — "They know food, and it shows in the first meeting"**

> **What do you like best?**
> The emission factors are built for food, not adapted to food. When we talked about our cocoa supply chain they already knew what a fermentation step was and why it mattered. Every other vendor we spoke to needed to be taught our industry before they could sell to us.
>
> **What do you dislike?**
> The interface looks like it was built by engineers, because it was. Reports are functional rather than beautiful. Loading a full year of data is slow.
>
> **What problem are you solving?**
> Deforestation regulation exposure on cocoa and palm inputs, and product footprints for two retail customers.

**Sustainability Lead, food manufacturing, 201 to 500 employees**

---

## 4. Review site, posted 21 April 2026, verified reviewer

**Rating: 3 / 5 — "Best measurement we have used. Stops exactly where the hard part starts."**

> **What do you like best?**
> Traceability. Every number goes back to a source, a method and a date, and the change log cannot be edited by an administrator. Our assurance provider said it was the first restatement in years he did not have to interrogate. That alone took his questions from eleven to two.
>
> **What do you dislike?**
> There is no reduction planning. You end up with an excellent picture of your footprint and no product support for deciding what to do about it. We have asked in every quarterly review for a year and the answer has been the roadmap each time. If your board has asked for a pathway to 2030, this is not the tool that produces it.
>
> **What problem are you solving?**
> Group level reporting across twelve brands, with two of them merging, and assurance.

**Sustainability Director, food and beverage, 1001 to 5000 employees**

## Notes from marketing

- We have five. We would like fifteen. Customer success says the customers most willing to speak publicly are the small ingredient producers, and the logos our sales team wants are the large groups, who all require legal review and mostly say no.
- Numbers two and five are written by us from conversations and then approved, which is normal practice and is how most vendor testimonials are produced. They read differently from the two review site entries, which are written by the customers themselves.
- Nobody has audited whether the phrases in the approved testimonials appear anywhere in a recorded customer call.
- We have no published testimonial from a grocer, from a non food customer, or from anybody in procurement.
- The four star and five star reviews came in unsolicited. The three star review came in unsolicited as well, one week after a quarterly business review.
- Reviewed again on 25 August. **Nothing has been added in four months**, and two of the five now describe accounts that are in trouble: the three star reviewer's complaint about reduction planning is the same sentence that came up in a renewal decision this month, and the group in number five is on the Q4 watchlist.
