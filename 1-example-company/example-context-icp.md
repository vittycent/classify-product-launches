## Firmographics

- **Geography.** Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Finland. A handful of Dutch and German accounts exist. No ICP definition has been written for them. [blank]
- **Employees.** 200 to 1500 for ingredient producers, 500 to 8000 for multi brand owners.
- **Structure.** Ingredient producers are frequently family owned or cooperative owned, one to three production sites, export heavy. Multi brand owners run 5 to 40 brands, usually with an acquisition history that left the data in different systems.
- **Products.** Physical food or beverage products with a recipe or a specification. If there is no bill of materials, there is no PCF, and the deal stalls in implementation.

## Triggers that make an account in market

Roughly in order of how often they show up in won deals.

1. **A customer sent a scorecard with a deadline.** Almost every ingredient producer deal starts here. A large brand owner or a grocer asks for product level numbers by a date, and the account has no way to produce them.
2. **First CSRD reporting cycle**, or an auditor pushing back on the assumptions in last year's spreadsheet.
3. **An SBTi FLAG target was committed to** and now needs land based data nobody has.
4. **A tender or range review requires a PCF** to even bid.
5. **The first sustainability hire lands**, or the person who owned the Excel model leaves and nobody can reopen it.
6. **EUDR exposure** on soy, palm, cocoa or coffee inputs.
7. **Investor or owner pressure**, most common at multi brand owners.

## Anti ICP

- **Anything outside food and beverage.** Our factors, schema and supplier logic assume food inputs. Note: the deal desk approved two non food deals last year under expansion pressure. Both are unhappy. See the open item in the company file.
- **Primary producers with no processing.** Farms and growers. The data sits with them, the budget does not.
- **Under about 30M EUR revenue.** No owner, no budget, and the work lands on someone with a day job.
- **One report, one time.** If the need is a single deliverable for a single tender, the consultancy wins and should. We churn these inside a year.
- **Decarbonization first.** If the buyer's opening ask is reduction planning rather than measurement, we are second best and they find out in about two quarters.

## What they use today

- **Spend data from the purchase ledger**, exported to Excel, multiplied by category average factors. Fast, defensible until questioned, and useless for showing a difference between two suppliers of the same ingredient.
- **Supplier questionnaires by email.** Response rates are low and the answers arrive in inconsistent units.
- **A consultancy report** from last year, usually a PDF, usually not repeatable.
- **Klimatly** for a quick number when a deadline is close.
- **Systems the data actually lives in.** ERP for spend, PLM or Excel for recipes and specifications, and supplier contacts in procurement's inbox.

**The access problem.** Procurement owns spend data. R&D owns recipes. Quality owns supplier contacts. Sustainability owns none of it and has no system of record. This is the single most common reason an implementation stalls, and it comes up in discovery far more often than pricing does.

## How each segment talks

The words differ enough that the same problem sounds like two problems.

**Ingredient producers.** "Our customers are asking." "The scorecard." "Spec sheet." "Raw material." "Batch." "Yield." "Our growers." "The co op." "We can't get anything back from the farms." They say customer meaning a large brand owner, never meaning a consumer.

**Multi brand owners.** "Portfolio." "The group." "SKU." "Category." "The retailer." "Our reduction target." "Assurance." "Double materiality." "What goes in the report." They say the business meaning a brand, and the group meaning head office.

## Transcript pack distribution

14 transcripts total, which is enough for Block 4 to produce a real count.

- **8 ingredient producers**, 8 separate accounts.
- **5 multi brand owners**, 5 separate accounts.
- **1 off ICP account**, a non food company that came in under expansion pressure. It is in the pack on purpose. It should not cluster with anything, and anyone who folds it into a vertical pattern has made the mistake the exercise is about.

Inside the ingredient producer set, one account is loud, articulate and repeats a complaint that is entirely real and entirely specific to them. Frequency and independence are the test, and that account fails it.
