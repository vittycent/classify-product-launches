## What we do

Rootline is a carbon accounting platform built only for food and beverage companies. We measure emissions down to the ingredient, so a customer can see that oranges from three different countries, grown under three different farming practices, carry three different footprints, and then do something about the difference.

## Market and category

**Category we sell into.** Carbon accounting and sustainability reporting software.

**What buyers actually call it.** Varies a lot. "Scope 3 tool", "CSRD tool", "footprint software", "the LCA thing". Sustainability teams rarely reach for the phrase carbon accounting first, and almost never in the first call.

**Where we play.** Nordics first: Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Finland. A small number of Dutch and German accounts. Leadership reads those as proof of European expansion. Sales does not read them that way yet.

**Why now.** CSRD reporting duties, SBTi FLAG targets for land based emissions, EUDR for deforestation linked inputs, and retailer scorecards that increasingly demand product level numbers from suppliers. Consumer and investor pressure sits behind all of it. Note: the CSRD scope rollback has given some accounts a reason to wait, and reps are hearing it.

## Product

Three parts.

**Core platform.** Corporate footprint across Scope 1, 2 and 3, built on food specific emission factors instead of generic spend averages.

**Rootline PCF.** Product carbon footprints for individual SKUs and recipes, resolved to ingredient, origin and farming practice.

**Rootline Supplier.** Supplier engagement, including reaching past the first tier down to growers. Chase, collect, validate, and fold what comes back into the customer's footprint.

### What we are genuinely good at

1. **Food and beverage depth.** We know how the industry works: recipes, co products, seasonality, sourcing that changes by quarter. We do not have to be taught the customer's business on the first call.
2. **Granular emission factors.** Ingredient level, not category averages. Origin and farming practice change the number, and we can show why.
3. **Data partnerships.** Partner data extends the factor library past what public databases carry, which is what makes ingredient level resolution hold up.
4. **Supplier engagement that reaches the farm.** Most tools stop at tier one. Ours is built to keep going, because in food the emissions are not at tier one.

### What we are not good at (do not oversell)

- **Decarbonization.** Measurement is strong. What to do next is thin. It sits as a feature inside the core platform, not as a module. Customers who bought us to reduce, not only to report, notice this within about two quarters.
- **User experience.** Known problem. Rebuild underway. No date. [status: open]
- **Anything outside food and beverage.** Factors, schema and supplier logic all assume food inputs. We do not serve these companies well and should not pretend otherwise.

## How we sell

- **Direct sales only.** No free trial, no self serve, no public demo. Every evaluation runs through a rep and a solutions call.
- **Typical contract.** 18k EUR per year. Band runs roughly 12k to 45k, with multi brand owners at the top.
- **Cycle.** Two to four months typical. Longer when procurement or a group sustainability function gets involved, which is most multi brand deals.
- **Terms.** Annual contracts, priced on revenue band plus the number of products modelled in PCF.

## Alternatives buyers weigh

**Verdanta.** Generalist enterprise carbon platform covering every industry. Better looking product, real decarbonization planning, much weaker on food. Wins when the buyer is a group function that also has to cover logistics, offices and retail.

**Klimatly.** Cheap, self serve, spend based. Fast to a number. The number does not survive an audit or a retailer challenge. Wins on price, and on speed when a deadline is close.

**Hedberg & Lund.** Nordic sustainability consultancy delivering footprints as a service. No software. Wins where the buyer has no internal capacity and wants a deliverable rather than a tool. Comes back every year at full price.

**Spreadsheets, spend data and supplier questionnaires.** The real incumbent, and the one we lose to most often. Someone builds it in Excel from purchase ledger spend, mails a questionnaire to suppliers, and fills the gaps with assumptions. It costs nothing and it holds until an auditor, a regulator or a customer asks where a number came from.
