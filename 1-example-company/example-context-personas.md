# Buying personas: Rootline

_Example context file, built for the PMM Alliance session. Fictional. See `example-context-company.md` and `example-context-icp.md`._

_Owner: PMM. Built from customer interviews in 2024, n=9, plus win/loss notes since. Not re interviewed. Titles vary more in the field than this file suggests._

**On the quotes.** Lines marked _[verbatim]_ came off a recorded call. Lines marked _[ours]_ were written by marketing and have been repeated back so often that people now think customers said them. Nobody has checked the rest. [status: open]

---

## 1. The Assigned Owner

**Sustainability and Quality Manager**, ingredient producer. Reports to the Operations Director, sometimes straight to the CEO.

**Role in the deal.** Champion, evaluator and end user, all at once. Does not sign.
**Who signs.** The CEO or the owner. At 50M to 500M revenue and family or cooperative ownership, 18k is a real decision that goes to the top.

**Background.** Food technologist, quality, or process engineering. Sustainability was added to their job, not hired for. Smart, respected internally, and has never bought software before. Does not know what a normal implementation looks like or what is reasonable to ask for.

**Measured on.** Keeping customers satisfied and audits clean. Sustainability is not in their formal objectives, which is part of the problem.

**Trying to do.** Answer the customer's request without being wrong in front of them.

**In the way.** No data below tier one. Buying through cooperatives, which return an average and not a farm. No authority to make suppliers respond. No time.

**What they say**

- "I got this on top of my normal job." _[verbatim]_
- "Our biggest customer sent a form and I have four weeks." _[verbatim]_
- "I don't know if the number I sent them last year was right." _[verbatim]_
- "We buy through a co op, so I get an average, not a farm." _[verbatim]_
- "If I go and ask the growers, they are going to ask me why." _[verbatim]_
- "We want to turn compliance into a competitive advantage." _[ours]_

**Stated ask.** "Can you give us a portal where our suppliers upload their own data?"

**Says no when.** The CEO looks at the price and says Excel worked last year. Or they realise they would personally have to run the thing and nobody has offered them time.

**Gets information from.** LinkedIn, industry association webinars, and whatever their customer's sustainability team tells them to use. One peer at another producer who they trust more than any vendor.

## 2. The Group Reporter

**Group Sustainability Manager**, multi brand owner. Sits in a group function of two to six people, reports to a Sustainability Director or to the CFO.

**Role in the deal.** Champion and evaluator, often the budget holder for tooling.
**Who signs.** Sustainability Director or CFO, usually against a budget line that already exists.

**Background.** Sustainability is the career, not the side task. Has bought software before, has run an RFP, and knows how to stall a vendor politely for a quarter. Reads the contract.

**Measured on.** The report landing on time and surviving assurance. Progress against a public reduction target.

**Trying to do.** Produce a number that holds up, across a portfolio that grew by acquisition and never got consolidated.

**In the way.** Brands on different systems. Spend based factors that cannot show a difference between two suppliers of the same ingredient. A land based target committed to before anyone checked whether the data existed.

**What they say**

- "The auditor is going to ask where this comes from." _[verbatim]_
- "It has to survive assurance." _[verbatim]_
- "Our number moved eleven percent and I could not explain to the board why." _[verbatim]_
- "I have twenty two brands and four of them are on a different ERP." _[verbatim]_
- "We committed to a FLAG target and now I need land data nobody has." _[verbatim]_
- "Marketing wants a claim on pack and I cannot give them one." _[verbatim]_
- "Sustainability is a boardroom priority for us." _[ours]_

**Stated ask.** "We need a dashboard the board can look at."

**Says no when.** A group tool has already been chosen for another purpose and IT will not add a second. Procurement and security review eats the quarter. Or the board reads the CSRD rollback coverage and asks why this is urgent.

**Gets information from.** Peers at other groups, sustainability networks, their assurance provider, consultants, industry press, and Verdanta's content, which is better than ours.

## 3. The Gatekeeper

**Sourcing or Procurement Manager.** Present in both segments. Rarely on the first call, always on the third.

**Role in the deal.** Blocker, and the single most underestimated person in the process. Owns spend data and the supplier relationships that Rootline Supplier depends on. Gains nothing from the purchase.

**Measured on.** Cost, continuity of supply, and supplier relationships that are often decades old.

**Trying to do.** Not add friction to a supplier base they are simultaneously negotiating price with.

**In the way.** Nothing. That is the point. This is the person in the way.

**What they say**

- "I am not sending my suppliers another survey." _[verbatim]_
- "These are relationships, not accounts." _[verbatim]_
- "Last time we asked, three of forty answered." _[verbatim]_
- "If this delays a delivery it is my problem, not sustainability's." _[verbatim]_
- "Who is paying for this?" _[verbatim]_

**Stated ask.** None. They were invited to a meeting they did not ask for.

**Says no when.** The request lands in the middle of a price negotiation, or nobody has told them what the suppliers get back.

**Gets information from.** [blank]

_Written from two calls and a lot of assumption. Thinnest persona in the file, and the one that decides the most deals._

## Who else turns up

- **IT and security.** Light at this contract size. A questionnaire, occasionally a DPA.
- **Finance.** Only at multi brand owners, and only if the budget line does not already exist.
- **The CEO or owner.** Common at ingredient producers, almost never at multi brand owners.
- **The customer who sent the scorecard.** Not in the room, and the most influential party in the deal.
