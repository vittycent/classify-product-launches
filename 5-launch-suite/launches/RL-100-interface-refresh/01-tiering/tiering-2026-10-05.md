## 3. Explainer for the PM

_For Pontus Grahn. What product marketing takes to the product manager._

**Tier 3: tell the customers it serves.** Every customer will see the new supplier view and export, so every customer needs to hear what changed and where things moved. It wins no deals and nobody asked for it, so a campaign would repeat the speed launch: big effort, no pipeline, and customers reading it as a promise.

**Why, in three reasons**

1. **Customers expect it** (10 of 10). Catching up still needs telling, but it is worth half of breaking new ground. Customers expect a product they can find their way around in.
2. **How many customers can use it** (10 of 10). Everyone on Supply and Group sees it on 10 November.
3. **Who needs it beyond our customers** (10 of 20, open). A big launch needs a market beyond the customers we already have. Frida scored it as a chance to change how the market sees Rootline; the agent found no deal or prospect that names it. This is the question for you.

**What would change it**

- 5 points under Tier 2, and the revenue gate keeps it at Tier 3 unless a deal names it.
- **Week 43:** if the reporting export moves to January, it becomes two launches, and each is scored on its own.
- After it ships, the quarterly calibration scores it again with its results (rubric v1.1).

**Questions for you**

1. Who beyond our customers would choose Rootline because of the refresh? Is there a deal it won or lost?
2. If the export moves to January, is that two launches, and what do we tell Sjöland?
3. What would the webinar on 12 November give customers that a CS briefing, an in app note and an email can't?
4. What did the tests show, beyond the sort that shipped on its own?

**If the tier holds:** two weeks before 10 November, led by you with Frida's approval, heard by every Supply and Group customer: a release note, an email to those customers, a CS briefing, an in app note.
