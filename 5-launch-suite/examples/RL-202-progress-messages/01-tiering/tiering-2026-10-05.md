## 3. Explainer for the PM

_For Jonas Wik. What product marketing takes to the product manager._

**Tier 4: log it, unless Frida decides the reason to move up is clear.** Progress messages fix something customers expect any software to do, for the customers who run long loads. It wins no deals and opens nothing new. It sits 2 points above the line where a launch tells the customers it serves, so the rule keeps it at the smaller launch unless there is a clear reason.

**Why, in three reasons**

1. **Customers expect it** (7 of 10). Catching up still needs telling, but it is worth half of breaking new ground. A long load with no sign of life is something customers notice; a message that it's working is something they expect.
2. **How many customers can use it** (6 of 10). Anyone who loads a full year of data or a large group: mostly groups and the larger producers.
3. **How strong the proof is** (6 of 15). Eight accounts asked and slow load tickets keep coming. But the customer who asked first says it doesn't solve her problem: she wants an email when the load is done.

**What would change it**

- 2 points above the Tier 3 line. Frida can move it up with a written reason (section 1); nothing in the scores is likely to move it further.
- An email when a load finishes or fails would change the proof for Fjordlaks, but it is not planned (Q1 at best).
- Nothing on the calendar triggers a rescore.

**Questions for you**

1. Fjordlaks asked first and wants an email, not a message. Is anything in step 2 for her, and what should the release note say to the people who start a load and walk away?
2. Which accounts raised slow loads since June? If this becomes Tier 3, support writes to each of them, and the groups among them need to hear that step 2 is not the consolidation fix.

**If the tier holds:** ship day, led by you: a changelog line and a mention in the monthly release note. If Frida moves it to Tier 3: two weeks of lead time, led by you with Frida's approval, an email to the customers who raised it and a CS briefing.
