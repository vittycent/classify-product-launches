## 3. Explainer for the PM

_For Samir Haddad. What product marketing takes to the product manager._

**Tier 2: tell customers and sales.** Scenario comparison is what customers have asked for most this year, and it helps win and keep groups. It is not ready yet and not yet proven, so for now the right size is a launch to customers and sales, not to the whole market, and only once there is a general release date.

**Why, in three reasons**

1. **How strong the proof is** (12 of 15). The stronger the proof, the more we can say in public without overpromising. Seventeen accounts, eleven support tickets, a lost evaluation, a renewal condition and two prototype rounds all point the same way. What's missing is the strongest kind: customers using it on a real decision.
2. **Fit with the company goals** (12 of 15). A release that moves a company goal deserves more. It is named in two: winning the groups, and customers acting on the number (23% against a target of 40%). The deals and renewals around it touch the other two.
3. **How many customers asked for it** (10 of 10). Requests matter, though one loud request alone shouldn't make a big launch. This is not one: it is the top idea on the feedback portal, seventeen accounts, asked for since April.

**What would change it**

- 10 points from the Tier 1 line. Two scores are open until we talk. On who needs it beyond our customers, the agent scores higher than Frida: four prospects ask for it. On how many customers can use it, the agent scores lower: only the Group package can. Settled the agent's way, the total is 63: still Tier 2.
- Proof from early access (from 8 to 10) and buyers starting to expect it (from 4 to 8) would add 7 points: not enough on their own.
- **The big deal rule would make it Tier 1 today.** Nordlys and Nordkost are waiting on it. But a bigger launch doesn't get Nordlys in: early access is full at four, and there is no general date. Deciding the override at the rescore on 16 November, with early access under way, costs nothing now.
- Dates that would trigger a new score: Tre Kronor's call on Tuesday 6 October (if they step back, a place opens); early access opening on 16 November; the January planning, where the CRO's question comes back; early access results at the end of January.

**Questions for you**

1. Of the four prospects asking, which would one material at a time actually serve? Tornedal and Trøndelag aren't groups on the Group package. (This settles the first open score.)
2. In the prototype sessions, when customers said "scenario", how many meant a costed plan? Henrik reads it as half. A count would settle it.
3. Will the general release stay in the Group package? Seven of the nine accounts behind the portal idea are single site producers. (This settles the second.)
4. If Tre Kronor steps back after Tuesday, does the place stay empty or go to another group?

**If the tier holds:** about 4 weeks before the general release, led by Frida with you, heard by existing customers and the current pipeline. Not before: early access stays quiet, as you asked.
