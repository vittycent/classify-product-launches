## 3. Explainer for the PM

_For Samir Haddad. What product marketing takes to the product manager._

**Tier 3: tell the customers it serves.** A one week change that customers have asked for since March, and the first proof this year that a customer will change how it works because of what Rootline shows it. It wins no deals by itself, so the right launch is personal and precise: every customer who asked hears it from us, not a campaign.

**Why, in three reasons**

1. **Fit with the company goals** (13.5 of 15). A release that moves a company goal deserves more. "Customers act on the number" is at 23% against 40%, and this is the first thing on the list to move it.
2. **How strong the proof is** (12 of 15). The strongest kind exists, from one customer: Havsfisk turned it on and its purchasing team asked why they hadn't been using the list before every supplier review.
3. **How many customers asked for it** (8 of 10). Requests matter, though one loud request alone shouldn't make a big launch. Seven accounts on the portal, sixteen support tickets, the same question every month since March.

**What would change it**

- Only the revenue gate keeps it at Tier 3. A deal that waits on it would lift the cap; none does.
- After 13 October, G4's monthly measure will show whether more customers open the ranking. That is calibration for the quarter, not a reason to rescore.

**Questions for you**

1. Two purchasing users want A to Z, and want it to stick. Is per user sort memory coming soon, or does the release note need to say how to switch back?
2. Havsfisk's purchasing team wants the list before every quarterly supplier review. Is the quarterly ranking page (RL-036, 19 October) their next step, and should both reach them together?

**If the tier holds:** two weeks of lead time, led by you with Frida's approval, heard by the customers who use the supplier screen. The release is 13 October, so the work should have started on 29 September. What fits in eight days: support writes to the seven accounts and everyone behind the sixteen tickets, a CS briefing, and a release note line that says how to sort A to Z.
