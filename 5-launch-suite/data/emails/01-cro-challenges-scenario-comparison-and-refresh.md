Henrik,

Fair questions. Short answers now, longer ones in January if you want them.

1. We picked what we could build with the people and the data we have. A cost per measure needs a cost library we don't have and a factor data team twice the size of Agnes's. Scenario comparison runs on the supplier data customers have already given us. It is the first half of a plan: which change moves the number. The second half, what it costs and by when, is reduction planning (RL-700), and that is still research.

On the calls: I read the same ones. Storhaug asked what happens after the number. Erik's own note says the supplier comparison would have answered it and he didn't show it. I don't read that as a vote for a cost library.

You may still be right that we are answering the smaller question. Early access will tell us: by the end of January, three of the four customers have used it on a real, signed supplier decision. If they haven't, I will say so before you have to.

2. The refresh doesn't win deals and I have never said it would. Every new screen we build in the old components, we build twice. And brand views, which is what Sjöland's renewal is about, cannot start until the refresh ships. It is retention and speed, not pipeline.

Pontus
