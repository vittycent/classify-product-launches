# Rootline launch rubric

_Fictional. Version 1.3, 9 October 2026; version 1 was set up on 4 October. Set up by Frida Engström (Product Marketing Manager) with the GTM Tiering Agent's setup interview, the weekend before scoring the Q4 releases (`transcripts/05-marketing-weekly-q4-launch-list.md`). Changes are recorded in `calibration-log.md`; this file always shows the current version._

**Read by:** the GTM Tiering Agent, and later the brief intake, the goal setter and the launch planner (the trigger table).

---

## 1. Why Rootline tiers

Launch effort at Rootline has followed how loud a request was and how long product worked on something, not who it helps or what it changes. In 2026 the faster loading release got 85 hours and 4,000 EUR of paid promotion and brought no leads; the supplier comparison got one hour and, since August, has closed or turned four accounts (`launch-history.md`). The rubric decides the size of a launch before anyone writes a word.

## 2. Tiers

| Tier | What it is at Rootline | Its job | A past Rootline example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gives Rootline an advantage competitors can't easily copy, such as a new category, and brings in revenue | Tell the market | The November 2025 packages, Supply and Group (the nearest Rootline has had) |
| 2 | Brings in revenue but catches Rootline up with the market: something customers expect | Tell customers and sales | Report templates for the revised reporting scope, June 2026, with the customer webinar |
| 3 | Serves Rootline's core customers: food and beverage producers and groups | Tell the customers it serves | Co product allocation for dairy, milling and meat, March 2026 |
| 4 | Makes the product better to use: quality, speed, less friction | Log it | Export file names with product and date, August 2026 |

## 3. Scoring questions

Each scored 0 to 10. A higher score always means a bigger launch.

| # | Question | Measures | 0 | 5 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | New category | Whether it opens a new market category | Strengthens Rootline's position in food and beverage carbon accounting | Complementary use cases in markets next to ours (purchasing, own brand claims) | Takes Rootline into a new category |
| 2 | Market need | Who needs it beyond current customers | Only the customer who asked | Food and beverage companies like ours that we don't serve yet | The market as a whole |
| 3 | Validation | What kinds of proof exist; how many asked is question 5 | No proof: a gut feeling; we build it to find out | Two kinds of proof agree, such as requests and a workaround customers pay for | Customers have used it, or several kinds of proof agree |
| 4 | Strategic signals | Fit with the 2026 company goals (`company-goals.md`). How important a release is to the business counts here, through a goal, and in no other question | No goal names it, directly or through a release it has to ship before (a deliberate bet scores 0 too) | Supports one goal, directly or by shipping before a release a goal names | Supports several goals |
| 5 | Customer request | Where the idea came from | Rootline's own idea; no customer asked | Our idea, and one or a few customers asked for something like it | Driven by requests from many customers |
| 6 | Market expectation | Whether customers expect it in our market | Not expected | Not expected, but customers would be delighted | Expected |
| 7 | Customer reach | How many current customers can use it | A few customers | More customers, in a few segments | Most customers |

**Importance belongs in one place.** If a release matters to the business and no goal says so, that is a goal to set, not a score to raise in validation, market need or market expectation.

**Proof, strongest first** (for validation): results from customers testing it; deals lost or stalled that named it; workarounds customers pay for (a consultant, extra staff, spreadsheets); requests in calls and emails, counted by customer; signals from inside Rootline (sales, customer success, support tickets, the feedback portal); gut feeling. Compare every release with what customers do without it, not only with Verdanta and Klimatly.

## 4. Weights

| Question | Weight | Max points | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| New category | 2 | 20 | A Tier 1 launch rests on an advantage competitors can't easily copy |
| Market need | 2 | 20 | A big launch needs a market beyond the customers Rootline already has |
| Validation | 1.5 | 15 | The stronger the proof, the more Rootline can say in public without overpromising |
| Strategic signals | 1.5 | 15 | A release that moves a company goal deserves more |
| Customer request | 1 | 10 | Requests matter, but one loud request alone shouldn't make a big launch |
| Market expectation | 1 | 10 | Catching up still needs telling, but it is worth half of breaking new ground |
| Customer reach | 1 | 10 | How many current customers can use it |
| **Total** | **10** | **100** | |

## 5. Gates

- **Development maturity.** Scoping (0), ready to test with customers (5), ready for customers to use (10). Below 5, the tier is provisional and the release is scored again when it is ready to test with customers.
- **Revenue.** Tier 1 or 2 only if the release opens a new revenue stream (a new package, add on or market) or helps win or expand deals. Otherwise Tier 3 at most.

## 6. Overrides

Up one tier at most, never down. The score stays as it is; Frida approves each override and it is logged with its reason in the assessment file.

- **Big deal:** an open opportunity of 30,000 EUR or more, or any multi brand group, that waits on the release according to the newest dated statement about the deal: the CRM notes (`pipeline.csv`), an email or a call, whichever is newest. A newer statement that the deal is on hold, or waits on something else, switches the override off
- **Market date:** a date in `market-calendar.md` that customers must meet, which the release helps them meet
- **Competitor:** Verdanta or Klimatly launched the same thing: the message says catching up, not first; the tier doesn't change

## 7. Tier lines

| Tier | Total |
|---|---|
| 1 | 70 or more |
| 2 | 50 to 69 |
| 3 | 30 to 49 |
| 4 | under 30 |

Within 3 points of a line, the agent flags it and Frida decides; the release stays in the lower tier unless the reason to move up is clear and written down.

## 8. Sources at Rootline

| Source | Trusted for | File |
|---|---|---|
| Roadmap (Linear export) | What is planned | `roadmap.csv` |
| Friday project updates, `#produkt`, internal meetings and email | What is really happening | `project-updates.md`, `slack/produkt.md`, `transcripts/`, `emails/` |
| Calls, emails, `#kundfeedback` | Customer evidence | `../../example-data/`, `../../4-asset-usage-agent/transcripts-w37-onward/`, `slack/kundfeedback-w37-w40.md`, `customer-demand.md` |
| Support desk and feedback portal | What customers struggle with and ask for | `support-tickets.csv`, `feedback-portal.csv` |
| CRM pipeline | Deals | `pipeline.csv` |
| Company goals, market calendar | Goals and dates | `company-goals.md`, `market-calendar.md` |
| Launch history | Past tiers and results | `launch-history.csv` |

**When sources disagree:** the newest dated statement from the item's owner wins; a newer conversation beats the roadmap tool; an automatic summary never beats its source (the call recording tool writes one for every call, and they are often wrong); if the disagreement would change the tier, the agent says so and asks.

## 9. What a tier sets in motion at Rootline

| Tier | How often | Lead time | Who leads | Who hears | Assets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | About once a quarter | 8 to 10 weeks | Frida, with the PM; Josefin Lundgren and Henrik Åkerlund sign off | New buyers, press, analysts, every customer | Positioning and messaging, pitch deck update, landing page, press, sales training (Tobias Rahm runs the demo part), campaign (email by Ebba Wahlberg, LinkedIn by Albin Sundin, paid), launch moment, demo video, customer proof, in app education, CS briefing (Karin Wallin), 30 days of follow up |
| 2 | One or two a month, combined into the quarter's story where possible | About 4 weeks | Frida, with the PM | Existing customers and current pipeline | Blog post, email to the customers it serves, sales one pager and briefing, LinkedIn, in app announcement, changelog, update to an existing web page |
| 3 | As needed | 2 weeks | The PM; Frida approves | The core customers it serves | Release note, email to those customers, CS briefing, in app note; support writes to every customer who raised it |
| 4 | As needed | Ship day | The PM | Customers who use it | Changelog line, a mention in the monthly release note |

## 10. Who decides, and when to look again

- Frida scores first, before seeing the agent's score, then gets the PM's input, then decides. Tier 1 also needs Josefin Lundgren and Henrik Åkerlund.
- Score again when scope is added or cut, the date moves into another quarter, results come in from customers testing it, it becomes ready to test with customers, a big deal or market date appears or falls away, or a competitor launches the same thing.
- **After a release ships:** at every quarterly calibration, every release from the last two quarters is scored again with its results (use, deals, tickets). A release that moves up a tier gets the launch it missed, as a second launch moment.
- Calibration: gaps of 3 or more points are discussed; each is fixed at its cause (a missing source, an anchor read two ways, a weight, or results after release); one change per review; every change in `calibration-log.md`; once a quarter, tiers are compared with `launch-history.csv`.
