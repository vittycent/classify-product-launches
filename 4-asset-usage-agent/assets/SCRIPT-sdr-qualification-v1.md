_This is a guide, not a recital. Learn the opener, cover the five areas in section 3, write down what you heard. If the call goes somewhere useful, follow it._

---

## 1. Before you dial

- Check what they downloaded or filled in, and when
- Check the CRM: have we spoken to this company before?
- Look up revenue and number of sites. If they are clearly under 30 million EUR, the call is a courtesy, not a qualification

## 2. Opener

> "Hi, this is [name] from Rootline. We do carbon footprinting for food and drink producers, product level and corporate level, and we're specialised in ingredients and primary production. You [filled in our form / downloaded our guide] last week. Is now still a good time?"

Why you are calling rather than emailing: "I'd rather understand what you're dealing with before I send you anything, otherwise I send you the wrong thing."

## 3. What to find out: role, tooling, timeline, budget, buying group

Tooling, timeline and budget are the core. Role comes first because it tells you how to read everything after it. The buying group comes last because by then they will tell you.

**Role.** Who are you talking to, and what can they decide?

- "Before I ask about the project, can you tell me a bit about your role? How did this land with you?"
- Listen for: their title, whether sustainability is a formal part of the job or something they picked up, who they report to, and whether they can sign anything
- Someone who inherited this from quality or operations is normal. Someone who cannot say who would approve it is a flag

**Tooling.** What do they use today?

- "What are you using today for your footprint, if anything?"
- Listen for: a spreadsheet, a consultant, Klimatly, Verdanta, nothing
- Ask about spreadsheets and consultants specifically; people forget to mention them

**Timeline and pressure.** What is driving this, and by when?

- "Is there a project, a regulation or a deadline behind this?"
- "What happens if it isn't done by then?"
- Listen for: a customer template or scorecard, a reporting year, an audit, a tender, an investor process, a new hire
- Pressure is what makes people buy. A real deadline with a consequence is worth more than any amount of interest. No pressure usually means no deal this year

**Budget and business case.** Is there money, and has anyone made the case for it?

- "Has budget been set aside for this, or approved by finance?"
- "Is your CFO or finance director involved? Have you spoken to them about it yet?"
- "Have you put together a business case, even an informal one?"
- Listen for: an approved budget, a finance person who already knows, a written case. "We haven't talked about money yet" is fine on a first call, but write it down

**Buying group.** Who else is involved in the decision?

- "Who else will be involved in deciding? Finance, procurement, IT, whoever owns the recipes?"
- "Who would sign the contract?"
- "Who else should be on the next call?"

Also ask: "Are you looking at anyone else?"

## 4. Qualify out quickly and kindly

Qualify out, and do not book a demo, when:

- **Under about 30 million EUR revenue.** Our customers are mostly above 40 million. Below 30, the implementation effort rarely pays back, and it lands on someone with a day job. Say so plainly
- **They want a one off report,** not a system. A consultancy is the better answer and we should say so
- **Outside food and beverage.** Our factors assume food inputs

If there is no pressure and no budget, do not force a demo. Offer something useful, keep in touch, and log it as nurture.

How to close it out: tell them we're probably not the right fit right now, and why (size, format or industry, not them). Leave the door open for when things change. Close the opportunity in the CRM the same day so nobody chases them.

## 5. If they ask

| They ask | Say |
|---|---|
| "What does it cost?" | Don't quote on a first call. If pushed: "It depends on what you need, but the range starts well below 50,000 euros a year. Entry level, product footprints only, is around 15,000." |
| "How are you different from Klimatly?" | "They're built to be fast and general. We're built to be specific to food: our factors are agricultural and marine, not adapted from a general database." |
| "How are you different from Verdanta?" | "They cover every industry. We only do food, which matters if food is most of your footprint." |
| "Can you do our [export scheme / packaging chemicals / compliance paperwork]?" | "That's very likely outside what we do. We're a carbon accounting platform, not a compliance documentation system. I'd rather tell you now than have the AE tell you next week." |
| "Can you just send me a deck?" | "I can, and I will after the call. It'll be the right one if I know what you're dealing with." |

## 6. The handoff

- Book a 30 minute discovery with the AE. Tell them: **"Half an hour, no deck, no demo unless you ask for one."**
- Ingredient producers go to Erik or Anders, multi brand groups and grocers to Sofia
- Ask what they want the AE to cover, and put it in the invite
- Send the invite the same day

## 7. What to write in the CRM

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| Role | Title, whether sustainability is formally theirs, who they report to, what they can decide |
| Tooling | What they use today |
| Timeline and pressure | The deadline, what is driving it, and what happens if it is missed |
| Budget and business case | Approved, set aside, or not discussed; whether finance or the CFO is involved; whether a business case exists |
| Buying group | Everyone else involved in the decision, and who signs |
| Competitors | Anyone else they are looking at |
| Size | Revenue band and number of sites |
| Next step | The booked discovery, or "qualified out" and why |
| Notes | Anything the AE should know before the call. Four lines is plenty |
