_How to use this deck: it is built to be cut. On a first call, show slides 2, 3 and 5, then go to the product. The rest is there when the buyer asks. Speaker notes say what to stress and what not to promise._

---

### S01: Carbon accounting built for food

**On the slide**

- Rootline
- Carbon accounting built only for food and beverage
- Measured at the ingredient. All the way to the farm.

**Speaker notes**

Do not read the slide. Ask what made them take the call.

---

### S02: Your customers are asking

**On the slide**

- Brand owners and grocers are asking suppliers for product level carbon numbers, with a date
- Targets in food depend on farm data almost nobody holds
- Deforestation rules put the origin of soy, palm, cocoa and coffee on the table
- Larger groups are preparing their first CSRD reports

**Speaker notes**

For most ingredient producers the first bullet is the whole reason for the call. Find out who is asking, for what, and by when. The regulation bullets matter for multi brand groups; for everyone else they are background.

---

### S03: In food, one line is the footprint

**On the slide**

- Dairy: the milk
- Bakery: the flour
- Brewery: the barley and the glass
- Everything else is a rounding error until that line is right

**Speaker notes**

This is the slide that tells the buyer we know their business. Ask them which line it is for them. Most tools spend the first year on the other forty lines.

---

### S04: Why spend based numbers don't work for food

**On the slide**

- A spend based footprint multiplies what you paid by an industry average
- Pay more for a better ingredient and your footprint goes up
- Fine for a first rough picture
- Not fine for a product number, a customer claim or a supplier decision

**Speaker notes**

Be fair to spend based tools: they are fast and they are what most companies start with. The point is that the number moves with your prices, not with your emissions.

---

### S05: Measured at the ingredient

**On the slide**

- Recipe, then ingredient, then origin, then farming practice
- The same ingredient from two countries, or grown two ways, is two different numbers
- Product footprints and your corporate footprint from one model

**Speaker notes**

Show, don't tell: go to the product here if you can. Open a recipe and click into the biggest line.

---

### S06: Where the numbers come from

**On the slide**

- Public life cycle databases for the baseline
- Licensed agricultural data from our partners for the detail
- About 2,800 ingredient level factors across the Nordic food base
- Every factor carries its source

**Speaker notes**

The partner data is what the buyer cannot get on their own. If they ask which partners, say we license from several agricultural data providers and that the source is visible on every factor. Do not name partners in the room.

---

### S07: Land use change, handled properly

**On the slide**

- When land is converted to grow a crop, the carbon released is attributed to what grows there afterwards
- Spread over twenty years, by convention
- Flagged on the line it belongs to, and shown when it moves
- Most tools leave it out or bury it in an average

**Speaker notes**

For palm, soy, cocoa and anything fed on imported soy, this can be the biggest single number on the page. Explain it before their auditor does.

---

### S08: Every line shows its quality

**On the slide**

- Primary data, regional average or global average, marked on every line
- A report with thirty percent primary data says so, on the face of it
- A number that states its own quality is worth more than a confident number that doesn't

**Speaker notes**

Buyers expect us to hide the gaps. We label them. This is the slide that wins the auditor and the customer's sustainability team.

---

### S09: All the way to the farm

**On the slide**

- In food, most emissions sit past the first tier: at the farm, the grower, the vessel
- Rootline Supplier sends a short, structured request in your name, not ours
- The system chases, you follow up on the ones that matter
- Every supplier gets their own number back

**Speaker notes**

The number back is the reason suppliers answer. Say it plainly: a farmer who gets their own figure, and can use it with every other buyer, has a reason to reply.

---

### S10: What we ask a dairy farm

**On the slide**

- Five data points
  1. Feed composition
  2. Manure management system
  3. Milk yield
  4. Energy source at the processing site
  5. Any footprint they have already calculated
- Under half an hour for most farms
- One figure back, theirs to keep

**Speaker notes**

Use the dairy example even with non dairy buyers: everyone understands a farm. For other categories the fields change, the principle does not: ask only for what moves the number.

---

### S11: How Rootline works

**On the slide**

- **Rootline Core:** your corporate footprint, Scope 1, 2 and 3, on food specific factors
- **Rootline PCF:** product footprints for your SKUs and recipes
- **Rootline Supplier:** supplier engagement, past the first tier

**Speaker notes**

Most customers start with PCF because a customer asked for product numbers. Mention that the corporate footprint comes out of the same model, so it is not a second project.

---

### S12: What it takes

**On the slide**

- First product footprints in six to eight weeks
- From you: a year of purchase data with quantities, your recipes, your supplier list
- From us: the model, the factors, and a solutions engineer who builds it with you

**Speaker notes**

Six to eight weeks is typical, not a promise. The thing that slows projects down is recipes that live in someone's head rather than in a system. Ask early.

---

### S13: Built by food people

**On the slide**

- Rootline started inside a question a Nordic dairy cooperative could not answer: which farms and which feed choices make our milk the number it is
- The first model was built for that cooperative's own supply chain
- Today: a dozen food producers across the Nordics, in dairy, oils and fats, and bakery

**Speaker notes**

Only show this if the buyer asks why we only do food, or who we are. It is a good story and a bad opening.

---

### S14: Next steps

**On the slide**

- A working session on your data: one product, one supplier list
- Your purchase export and two or three recipes
- A proposal within a week

**Speaker notes**

Book the working session before the call ends. Ask who else needs to be in it: whoever owns the recipes is usually the person who decides how fast this goes.
