## 1. What changed since v1

Eight months ago we were a food carbon accounting tool with a dozen customers and a good story about farms. We now have around forty customers, two packages, a reporting season arriving for our largest accounts, and a clearer idea of why people buy us.

v2 does four things:

1. Leads with assurance and defensibility, because that is what our largest buyers are being asked for this year
2. Adds a new headline, taken from a customer: **turn compliance into a competitive advantage**
3. Builds the packages (Supply and Group) into the positioning
4. Retires lines that no longer earn their place: land use change as a headline, the founders' story, and "one line is the footprint"

Use this document when you write a deck, an email, a blog post or a proposal. If something here is wrong, tell the PMM rather than working around it.

## 2. Positioning statement

**For** food and beverage producers and groups who have to stand behind a carbon number in front of an assurance provider, a customer or a board,

**Rootline is** the carbon accounting platform built only for food and beverage,

**that** gives you a number you can defend: measured at the ingredient, traced to its source, and assured.

**Unlike** generalist platforms, which treat agriculture as a category average, and spend based tools, which measure your prices rather than your emissions,

**we** combine food specific data, experience of what holds up under scrutiny, and a full audit trail, so every number and every claim built on it can be explained.

## 3. The headline

> **Turn compliance into a competitive advantage.**

It comes from a customer, a group sustainability manager at a mid sized Nordic food group, in February 2026: "For the first time we can stand behind our environmental claims. Rootline helps us turn compliance into a competitive advantage." The quote has been approved by the customer for anonymised use and goes on the website in March.

Use it as the headline on the website, the deck title slide and campaign copy. In a live conversation, don't lead with it: lead with the buyer's problem, and let the headline sum up where the conversation ends.

## 4. The one sentence version

Rootline is carbon accounting built only for food and beverage: numbers you can defend, from the ingredient to the assurance report.

## 5. Why now (February 2026)

In the order that matters most to our largest buyers this year:

1. **First CSRD reports, with limited assurance.** Groups in scope are closing their first reporting year and meeting their assurance provider for the first time
2. **Customers keep asking.** Brand owners and grocers want product level numbers from their suppliers, with a date
3. **Deforestation rules.** The origin of soy, palm, cocoa and coffee is now a question with a deadline
4. **Targets.** Food companies setting targets need data from farms they don't own

## 6. Messaging pillars

### 6.1 Assurance ready

- **Every number traces back** to its activity data, its system extract and its factor, with the factor's **source, vintage, geography and method**
- **Every Scope 3 category is screened,** and every exclusion documented with a reason and a name
- **Restatements are split by cause:** factor updates, corrections to your own data, methodology changes
- **Closed periods are locked,** and the change log can't be edited by anyone

### 6.2 A number you can defend

- **There is no single right number.** Different vendors use different datasets, boundaries and judgement calls. What matters is a number that fits your business and that you can explain, including any claim you build on it
- **Every line shows its quality:** primary data, regional average or global average
- **Gaps are filled with credible assumptions,** from food specific data, clearly marked and explained, rather than generic averages
- A number that states its own quality is worth more than a confident number that doesn't

### 6.3 Built for food, measured at the ingredient

- **Built only for food and beverage.** Factors, data model and supplier logic all assume agricultural inputs
- **Purchased goods are 80 to 95 percent** of a food company's footprint, so that is where the precision goes
- **Ingredient level factors, by origin and farming practice,** from public databases plus licensed partner data: about 5,000 factors today

### 6.4 All the way to the farm

- **Supplier engagement** past the first tier, to the farm, grower and vessel, sold as an add on in blocks of 25 active suppliers a year
- **Your company asks, Rootline processes.** The request goes out in the customer's name and introduces Rootline as the data partner, with a clear promise on how supplier data is handled
- **Suppliers get their own figure back.** It is the main reason they answer
- **It's a joint project.** We provide the template, the system and the reminders. The customer's team owns the supplier relationships and the follow up

### 6.5 Reduction starts with supplier level data

- **You cannot reduce what you cannot see.** In food, reduction happens in sourcing, and a sourcing decision needs supplier level numbers, not a category average
- Measurement isn't a step you finish before reduction starts. In food, it is what makes reduction possible
- Be precise about what we do today: we give customers the evidence a reduction plan has to be built on. We do not produce the plan (section 12)

### 6.6 The first request is never the last

- The customer who asks for product footprints this year is followed by a second customer, a bank or an auditor next year
- Product and corporate footprints come from one model, so the next request starts from a base, not from zero

## 12. What we don't do

Say these plainly, early, in the same words every time:

- **Reduction planning.** Scenario modelling today is single product what ifs only. There is no group level supplier swap, no measure library, no cost curves and no pathway to a target year. Don't describe any of them as available
- **Packaging chemistry.** We calculate the carbon of packaging materials. We don't assess whether a film or a coating is compliant with food contact or chemical rules
- **Offsets.** We measure footprints. We don't judge the quality of carbon credits
- **Anything outside food and beverage.** Our factors and supplier logic assume food inputs. If the buyer makes packaging, chemicals or anything else, tell them we are the wrong tool
- **Resold branded goods.** A grocer's footprint for branded products depends on the brand owner's disclosure. We don't pretend to close that gap with category averages
- **The interface.** A refresh is under way, starting with the supplier view and the reporting export. Don't promise dates
