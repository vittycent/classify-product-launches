### S05: In food, purchased goods are the footprint

**On the slide**

- Purchased goods and services: typically **80 to 95 percent** of a food company's footprint
- Upstream transport: a few percent
- Your own sites: usually less than you'd expect

**Speaker notes**

This is why site efficiency projects rarely move the total. Most of the footprint is in what you buy, which is where the rest of the deck goes.

### S13: Why spend based numbers don't hold up: a worked example

**On the slide**

| | Supplier A | Supplier B |
|---|---|---|
| Rapeseed oil, same specification | 1,000 EUR a tonne | 1,150 EUR a tonne (certified) |
| Spend based footprint | 100 | **115** |
| Activity based footprint | 100 | **92** |

- Pay 15 percent more for a better oil and a spend based number says your footprint rose 15 percent
- An activity based number says it fell
- A spend based number measures your prices, not your emissions

**Speaker notes**

Be fair to spend based tools: they are fast and fine for a first rough picture. Use this slide when a buyer is comparing us with a cheaper, spend based option, or wants to put a number on a pack.
