## 1. The question on your desk

If you run a dairy, there is a good chance a customer has recently asked you for the carbon footprint of your products. Maybe it arrived as a template from a retailer, maybe as a line in a supplier review. Either way, it came with a deadline.

Most teams start with what they can see: the plant, the trucks, the packaging. It makes sense. That is the data you already have. It is also rarely where the story is.

## 2. Follow the milk

In food, the footprint tends to gather in one place. For a dairy, that place is the milk, and the milk's story starts on the farm, long before it reaches your intake.

That changes where the effort should go. A small improvement in how well you understand your farms will move your footprint more than a big improvement anywhere else.

## 3. What happens on the farm matters most

A handful of things on a farm shape the number: what the herd eats, how manure is handled, how productive the herd is, and the land itself.

The last one surprises people. Sometimes the biggest line in a dairy footprint isn't a cow at all. It's the ground the farm sits on.

Where feed was grown matters too. Land that was cleared to grow a crop carries that history into everything made from it, and that history can sit in your footprint for years. A good carbon model accounts for it openly, instead of burying it in an average.

## 4. Getting data from farms doesn't have to be a struggle

Many dairies have sent a questionnaire to their farms and heard very little back. It is not because farmers don't care. It is usually because the request was long, unfamiliar and gave them nothing in return.

The dairies that get answers tend to follow a few simple principles:

- **Ask for less.** A few questions a farmer can answer quickly beat a long form.
- **Ask as yourself.** A request from the dairy a farmer already sells to lands very differently from one sent by a software company.
- **Give something back.** Every farm that shares its data should get its own figure in return, to keep and to use with anyone else who asks.

## 5. Honest beats perfect

You won't have data from every farm in your first year, and that is fine.

The footprints buyers trust most are not the ones with the lowest number. They are the ones that are clear about what they are made of: which parts come from your own farms and which come from averages. A number that explains itself holds up better than one that just looks confident.

## 6. A simple way to start

1. **Understand the ask.** Which products, which boundary, which deadline.
2. **Map your supply.** Know which farms send you the most milk.
3. **Start where it counts.** Begin with the farms that make up most of your volume, and build from there.

---

**Key takeaways**

- In dairy, the footprint lives mostly on the farm, not in the plant.
- Land and feed choices can matter more than people expect.
- Farmers share data when the request is short, familiar and gives something back.
- Be clear about what your number is made of. Buyers notice.
