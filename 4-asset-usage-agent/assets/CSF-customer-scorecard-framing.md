## What changed

This spring the thresholds for sustainability reporting were raised. Many mid sized food and beverage companies now fall into a later wave, or out of scope, depending on how their country implements the change.

For some, that has been presented as good news. For most producers, it changes less than it seems.

## What didn't change

**Your customers.** Many of the brand owners, grocers and food groups you sell to are still in scope, and your products are part of their footprint. If you sell to a company that still reports, its obligation reaches you as a supplier requirement: in a template, a tender or a clause in your next contract.

**Their deadlines.** A customer's scorecard comes with its own date, and it doesn't move when the law does.

## What the scorecards ask for, this year and next

- **This year,** most retailer and brand templates still accept a spend based number, as long as the method is stated
- **They are tightening.** Product level numbers, activity based methods and a share of primary data are moving from "nice to have" into the templates themselves
- **Being early is cheaper.** A model built once answers this year's template and next year's, and the corporate footprint comes from the same data

## Where your number gets read

When a scorecard reaches a category or range review, your footprint sits in a row next to price, volume and your competitors. It is read quickly, by someone who won't ask a follow up question.

What decides how it reads is the line beside it: how much of the number is measured, and how much is an average. A number that states its own quality holds up in that room. A low number with nothing underneath it doesn't.

## What to do now

1. **List the customers asking,** with what they asked for and by when
2. **Answer this year's template with a number that states its quality,** not just a figure
3. **Build the model once,** so next year's request starts from a base
4. **Start supplier data where your number is biggest,** before the templates require it
