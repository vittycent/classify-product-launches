## Slides and talk track

### S01: The reporting scope has changed. What it means for your programme

**Talk track**

Thank you for joining. Thirty minutes, three parts: what changed, what didn't, and how to talk about your programme inside your company. Then your questions.

---

### S02: What changed

**On the slide**

- The size thresholds for sustainability reporting were raised this spring
- Many mid sized companies move to a later wave, or out of scope, depending on national implementation
- The largest groups still report, on the timeline they expected
- Details are still being settled country by country

**Talk track**

We're not lawyers, and we won't tell you whether you're in or out: your auditor or legal adviser can. What we can say is that a good share of our customers will find the date on their reporting obligation has moved, or gone.

---

### S03: What didn't change

**On the slide**

- The companies you sell to: many of them are still in scope, and their supply chain is part of their number
- Banks and investors asking about climate risk
- Targets your company has already set, publicly or internally
- Any environmental claim you make on a pack, in a tender or on your website still has to hold up

**Talk track**

For most of you, the regulation was one reason among several. Look at the other three: in most of your companies at least one of them is getting louder, not quieter.

---

### S04: The question you'll be asked

**On the slide**

> "If reporting is optional now, why are we still paying for this?"

**Talk track**

Somebody in your company will ask this, probably your CFO, possibly at renewal time. It's a fair question and it deserves a better answer than "because we started". The next three slides are how we'd answer it.

---

### S05: Start from what the data is used for

**On the slide**

- Which customers have asked for your numbers in the last twelve months, and which will ask next
- Which decisions the numbers have informed: a supplier, a recipe, a claim, a target
- Who else in the company uses them: sales, purchasing, finance, the board

**Talk track**

If you can name the uses, the conversation is about value, not about compliance. If you can't name any yet, that's worth knowing now rather than at renewal, and your CSM can help you find the first one.

---

### S06: What stopping would cost

**On the slide**

- Rebuilding the model later: the data collection, the supplier requests, the history
- Answering customer requests by hand, or with numbers that move with your prices
- Losing the year on year comparison your targets depend on

**Talk track**

A footprint that's kept up to date is cheap to maintain and expensive to restart. The years you've already built are the part you can't buy back.

---

### S07: A one page internal brief

**On the slide**

1. What we've built: products, entities, suppliers covered
2. What we use it for: the uses from S05, with names and dates
3. What changed, and what didn't: S02 and S03 in our own words
4. What stopping would cost: S06, for our company
5. What we propose for next year

**Talk track**

This is the page to write before anyone asks. Your CSM will send you a template after the session, and they'll fill in the first two sections with you if you'd like.

---

### S08: What we're doing

**On the slide**

- Nothing in your contract changes because of the new thresholds
- Your CSM will go through your uses at your next review, and help with the brief
- We'll keep you posted as countries implement the change

**Talk track**

We'd rather you heard this from us than worked it out alone. If your situation has changed a lot, tell your CSM early: we'd rather adjust your plan than surprise either of us at renewal.

## Questions from the live session

### Q1: We've fallen out of scope. Should we still do a full corporate footprint this year?

If a customer, a bank or a target depends on it, yes. If none of them does, talk to your CSM about focusing on the product footprints your customers ask for, and keeping the corporate model up to date at a lower effort.

### Q2: Our CFO wants to pause everything until the rules are final. What do we say?

That pausing is a decision too. The model and the supplier history take time to rebuild, and customer requests don't pause. The brief on S07 is written for exactly this conversation.

### Q3: Will Rootline change its pricing because of this?

Not because of the thresholds. Your package stays as it is until renewal, and your CSM will review what you use before then.

### Q4: Do our customers' requests count as a reporting obligation?

Not a legal one. But a customer that needs your numbers for its own report will keep asking for them, deadline or not.
