_How to use this deck: nobody needs all 22 slides. Use it to set the stage before a demo: pick three to five slides for the buyer in front of you, then go to the product. For a group facing its first report, start with S02, S03, S12 and S18. For a producer answering customer requests, start with S05, S06, S15 and S19. If the buyer's reporting obligation has moved or gone, use S23 and send the customer scorecard framing (CSF). It is not for SDR calls; the qualification script covers those._

### S02: Your first CSRD report is here

**On the slide**

- Larger food groups are closing their first CSRD reporting year, with limited assurance
- Your customers want product level numbers, with a date attached
- Deforestation rules put the origin of soy, palm, cocoa and coffee on the table
- Targets in food depend on data from farms you don't own

**Speaker notes**

For groups in scope, the first bullet is the conversation. For producers outside scope, go straight to the second bullet and then to S05: a customer's request is what most of them are dealing with.

### S03: What your assurance provider will ask

**On the slide**

1. Where did this number come from?
2. What's in the boundary, and what's out?
3. What changed since last year, and why?
4. How much of it is measured rather than estimated?
5. Could anyone have changed it after the fact?

**Speaker notes**

Walk through the five, then tell them the rest of the deck answers each one. The group CSRD blog post covers the same ground if they want something to forward.

### S05: In food, purchased goods are the footprint

**On the slide**

- Purchased goods and services: typically **80 to 95 percent** of a food company's footprint
- Upstream transport: a few percent
- Your own sites: usually less than you'd expect

**Speaker notes**

This is why efficiency projects at their own sites rarely move the total. The precision has to go where the emissions are, in what they buy.

---

### S06: Measured at the ingredient

**On the slide**

- Recipe, then ingredient, then origin, then farming practice
- The same ingredient from two countries, or grown two ways, is two different numbers
- Product footprints and the corporate footprint come from one model

**Speaker notes**

If the demo environment is up, open a product and click into its biggest line. It does more than this slide can.

---

### S07: Where the numbers come from

**On the slide**

- Public life cycle databases for the baseline
- Licensed agricultural data from our partners for the detail
- About 6,400 ingredient level factors
- Every factor carries its **source, vintage, geography and method**

**Speaker notes**

Those four attributes are what an assurance provider checks first. In the demo, click on a factor and show all four.

---

### S08: There is no single right number

**On the slide**

- Two vendors can give you two different footprints, and neither is necessarily wrong
- Datasets, boundaries, allocation and missing data all involve judgement
- What matters is a number that fits your business, and that you can explain, along with every claim you build on it

**Speaker notes**

Use this when a buyer has a number from somewhere else and wants to know which one is right. The honest answer is that it depends on the choices behind it; our job is to make those choices visible and defensible.

---

### S09: Every number traces back

**On the slide**

- From the report line to the activity data, to the system extract it came from, to the factor applied
- Every manual change is logged: who, when and why
- Nothing is overwritten. The original stays next to the change

**Speaker notes**

Let the buyer pick the line to trace. It lands harder when they choose it.

### S10: Every category screened

**On the slide**

- All fifteen Scope 3 categories screened, not just the big ones
- Categories that don't apply are recorded as not applicable, with a reason and a name
- Nothing left blank for an assurance provider to wonder about

**Speaker notes**

For food, most of the fifteen are small. The point is that every category has an answer.

### S11: Restatements you can explain

**On the slide**

- Every change between years split into three causes:
  1. Factor updates
  2. Corrections to your own data
  3. Methodology changes
- Each one disclosed the way it should be

**Speaker notes**

Ask whether they restated last year, and whether anyone could explain why. Groups that had to will lean forward here.

### S12: Ready for your assurance provider

**On the slide**

- Close a reporting period and its numbers and factors are locked
- Restating a prior year is a deliberate step, with old and new figures shown together
- The change log is append only: no one can edit or delete an entry, including us
- Read access for your assurance provider, so they can check it themselves

**Speaker notes**

This is the slide for annual reporting. If the assurance provider is on the call, offer to show them the change log directly. Two assurance seats come with Rootline Group; on Supply they are an add on.

### S13: Every line shows its quality

**On the slide**

- Primary data, regional average or global average, marked on every line
- Where supplier data is missing, credible assumptions from food specific data, marked and explained
- A number that states its own quality is worth more than a confident number that doesn't

**Speaker notes**

Buyers expect us to hide the gaps. We label them, and we can explain every assumption we made to fill one.

### S14: Reduction starts with supplier level data

**On the slide**

- You can't reduce what you can't see
- In food, reduction happens in sourcing
- A sourcing decision needs numbers by supplier, not a category average

**Speaker notes**

Be precise about what we do today. We give customers the evidence a reduction plan is built on; we don't build the plan. If they ask for reduction planning, say so plainly and point to S21.

### S15: Start with the suppliers that carry the number

**On the slide**

- Rank suppliers by emissions, not by spend
- In most food companies, a small group of suppliers carries most of the footprint
- Start there, not with the whole supplier list

**Speaker notes**

Most buyers picture a questionnaire going to every supplier they have. Show them the ranked list instead: it makes the work look manageable and tells them where precision pays off.

### S16: All the way to the farm

**On the slide**

- In food, most emissions sit past the first tier: at the farm, the grower, the vessel
- Your team sends a short request in your company's name, introducing Rootline as your data partner
- Automatic reminders, and every supplier gets their own figure back
- Supplier engagement is an add on, in blocks of 25 active suppliers a year

**Speaker notes**

Be clear it's a joint project: we provide the template, the system and the reminders, and their team owns the supplier relationships and the follow up. The figure back is the reason suppliers answer.

### S19: Case study: Bryggeri Kvarnen

**On the slide**

A Swedish brewery, customer since September 2025. It needed product footprints for its range ahead of a reporting deadline.

| Where the footprint of a can of lager sits | Share |
|---|---|
| Malt | About a third |
| Packaging, mostly the can | About a third |
| Energy in brewing | About a fifth |
| Distribution and smaller inputs | The rest |

- Packaging turned out to be as big as the malt
- The fastest lever was the recycled content of the can, a figure their can supplier already had
- Next: malting energy, with their maltster

**Speaker notes**

Use it with any beverage or packaged goods buyer. The point for every producer is the same: the split is rarely where they expect, and the fastest lever can sit with a supplier who already has the figure. Kvarnen has approved use of their name.

- What we're working on:

|---|---|---|

| Faster data loading | Brand level read only views | Interface refresh: the rest of the product |
| | **Scenario comparison: swap a supplier, see the group number (in design)** | Reduction planning: exploring |

- Themes, not commitments. Order and timing can change

**Speaker notes**

Buyers putting years of supplier data into a platform want to know it will be there, and where it's heading. Answer with the facts on this slide, not adjectives. Scenario comparison is in design: don't demo it, don't describe it as available, and don't give a date. Today's product does single product what ifs only. If a buyer needs a date to decide, take it back to product and come back in writing.

### S22: What it takes, and next steps

**On the slide**

- Product and corporate footprints in four to six weeks
- From you: a year of purchase data with quantities, your recipes, your supplier list
- From us: the model, the factors, and a solutions engineer who builds it with you
- Next: a working session on your own data, one product or one entity, and a proposal within a week

**Speaker notes**

Four to six weeks assumes the data exists and someone on their side has time for it. Ask who that person is before you promise a date, and book the working session before the call ends.

### S23: The reporting rules moved. Your customers' scorecards did not.

**On the slide**

- Reporting thresholds were raised this spring: many mid sized producers are now in a later wave, or out of scope
- The brand owners, grocers and groups you sell to are often still in scope, and pass the requirement on to their suppliers
- This year's templates accept spend based numbers. Next year's are asking for more

**Speaker notes**

Use this when the buyer's case was built on CSRD and the obligation has moved. Ask which customers are asking them for numbers, and by when. Then send the customer scorecard framing (CSF).
